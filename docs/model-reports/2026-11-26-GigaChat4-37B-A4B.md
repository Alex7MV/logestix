**Verdict at a glance:** GigaChat4-37B-A4B — 37B total / 4B active, a hybrid of
GatedDeltaNet (24 layers) and DSA-sparse gated MLA (8 layers) with a 192-expert
top-8 MoE — runs locally on a single consumer RTX 5090 in our C++/CUDA engine.
The whole expert set fits the card beside the dense backbone, so decode is not
PCIe-bound: **59.1 tokens/s** at the canonical 2 048-token protocol (TTFT
449 ms), 5× the throughput of the 432B GigaChat3.5 sibling on the same card.
SelfCheck is bit-exact across three cycles (`logits_hash 20148869d4404e55`,
top-1 `Paris`), the native chatml-v5 GCML tool round-trip returns
`get_weather {"city":"Paris"}`, and FSI/CODE/DET are 3/3 each. The three QA
goldens are **template-derived** (the FP8 source needs a host-side dequant to
74 GB BF16 to execute); they pin the converted path, and the source-side
agreement is established separately by a teacher-forced per-layer comparison
against the reference implementation.

## Publish-ready post

### GigaChat 4 37B-A4B on one RTX 5090

We ported Sber's GigaChat4-37B-A4B into our Blackwell inference engine. It
keeps the hybrid backbone of GigaChat 3.5 — GatedDeltaNet linear attention on
24 of its 32 layers — and adds DeepSeek-style sparse attention to the 8
full-attention layers: a lightning indexer scores every cached key and lets
each query attend to only its top 2 048. Above a 2 048-token context that is a
real change of shape, not a knob; below it the selection is the identity.

The 39 GB FP8 checkpoint becomes a 23 GB NVFP4 tree. Unlike the 432B sibling,
which streams 219 GB of routed experts from host memory through an LRU, this
model's entire 19.7 GB expert set lives on the card next to the dense weights,
so a decode step is compute-bound rather than PCIe-bound. It decodes at
**59.1 tokens/s** at 2 048 generated tokens with a 449 ms first token.

#### What the checkpoint forced into the engine

1. **The gated norm gates on the *scaled* state.** Every normalization is a
   `GatedNorm`: an RMSNorm whose output is multiplied by a rank-16
   `silu → sigmoid` gate. The 3.5 checkpoint computes that gate from the
   normalized vector; the 4.0 checkpoint computes it from the vector **already
   multiplied by its scale**. Reading 4.0 the 3.5 way is not a rounding
   difference: on the checkpoint's own `post_self_attn_layernorm` (whose weight
   has mean ≈ 0) the two forms differ by a factor of 36, and the residual
   stream grows at every layer instead of staying put. We found it by
   dumping the engine's per-layer residual stream and comparing it against the
   reference implementation: cos 0.076 before, 0.997 after.
2. **A sparse indexer with its own projections, its own rope, and its own
   tie-breaking.** The indexer is a second, tiny attention: a query from the
   α-scaled query latent (8 192 × 1 536), a single 128-wide key per position
   with a plain RMSNorm and **non-interleaved** rotary embedding (the main
   attention uses interleaved), a per-head weight vector, and the score
   `Σ_h w_h · relu(q_h·k)` scaled by `1/√128`. Selection is exact top-k with a
   lowest-index tie fill, so two runs always agree. Below 2 048 keys the
   selector returns the full causal prefix, which is what makes short-context
   parity attainable at all.
3. **Two RoPE conventions and a YaRN factor of 32.** θ = 1e5 with YaRN over a
   32 768-token pretrained window. The cos/sin multiplier is exactly 1.0 (the
   two mscale values are equal), while the attention scale carries the squared
   YaRN mscale: `(0.1·ln32+1)²/√192 = 0.130861`. Applying the multiplier twice,
   or reusing the 3.5 factor of 8, silently mis-scales long-range attention.
4. **`α_q`/`α_kv` latents.** The query and KV latents are scaled by
   `√(hidden/rank)` *before* `q_b_proj`/`kv_b_proj`, and the α-scaled query
   latent is also what feeds the indexer.
5. **Logit soft cap 30.** `30·tanh(x/30)` on the F32 logits, before sampling
   and before the recorded logits — otherwise every gate compares a different
   quantity than the served model.
6. **The GDN output norm is not zero-centred** (plain scale, sigmoid gate
   ×2.0), unlike the 3.5 checkpoint, and the shared expert is added to the
   *pre-MoE* residual.
7. **Mixed source precision.** Some linears are FP8 with block-128 scale tiles,
   others are plain BF16 in the same checkpoint (the attention gate, the shared
   experts, the dense layer-0 MLP, the indexer's `wk`/`weights_proj`). A
   converter that keys quantization off the tensor *name* rather than off the
   presence of a scale tensor will silently pass BF16 payloads through the FP8
   dequantizer.

#### What we did not measure, and why

- The three QA goldens (FSI/CODE/DET) are **template-derived**: executing the
  source means dequantizing 39 GB of FP8 to BF16 (~74 GB) and running the
  vendor architecture on the host. Instead we ran a directed teacher-forced
  comparison (below), which localizes a defect to a layer rather than to "the
  answer is wrong".
- No 1M-token context: the session cap is 4 096 (rope and index tables are
  sized to it). DSA above 2 048 keys is exercised by the kernel tests and by
  the 2 048-token perf run, not by a long-document eval.
- MTP (the checkpoint's own single prediction layer) and the shipped
  `dflash_draft` speculative drafter are stored and counted, not applied.
- Batch > 1, multi-GPU and tensor parallelism are out of scope for this card.

#### Notes for the model authors

1. `config.json` declares `"use_cache": false` while the architecture is
   stateful (`_is_stateful = True`) and `max_position_embeddings` is 1 048 576.
   Three fields that disagree about whether the model is used with a cache is a
   trap: a caller that trusts `use_cache` decodes one token at a time from
   scratch.
2. `quantization_config.ignored_layers` lists `self_attn.conv1d`,
   `self_attn.A_log`, `self_attn.dt_bias`, `mlp.gate.weight`,
   `indexer.wk`, `indexer.weights_proj` — but **not**
   `self_attn.indexer.wq_b`, which is the only indexer tensor stored in FP8.
   The list is therefore not a reliable classifier of source precision; the
   per-tensor `weight_scale_inv` sibling is.
3. The chat template's `reasoning` default is `True`, which opens a `<think>`
   block. The closing `</think>` is a **special** token, so any consumer that
   decodes generated ids through a tokenizer that drops specials loses the
   answer boundary and will read the model's chain-of-thought as its answer. We
   serve the instant mode (`reasoning=False`) for single-answer prompts and
   gate on that frame; a downstream caller that wants the reasoning channel
   must cut on the token id, not on decoded text.
4. `dflash_draft/` is a complete second model directory (its own
   `config.json` and `model.safetensors`) with no pointer from the parent
   `config.json`. A tool that globs `*.safetensors` under the checkpoint will
   merge the drafter into the target.

### The variant refactor (why the 3.5 row is in this commit)

One pipeline serves both GigaChat checkpoints; everything a model does
differently is data in `GcPipelineDims` (`norm_w_offset`,
`norm_gate_from_scaled`, `gdn_w_offset`, `logit_softcap`, `attn_scale`, the
YaRN parameters, the DSA geometry). The 3.5 entry points
(`RunZeroCenteredGatedNorm`, `RunGdnOutputNorm`) are kept and are asserted
bit-identical to the parameterised form on device
(`test_gigachat_moe_37b_devkernels`, "offset 1 must reproduce the 3.5 kernel
bit-exactly"), and the whole 3.5 row (30 targets) stays green.

### Source-agreement probe (operator-approved scope)

`tools/gigachat4_layer_probe.py` loads the checkpoint's prefix (FP8
block-dequantized with its scale tiles), runs the reference
`modeling_gigachat4.py` on a 24-id prefix, and compares the residual stream at
position 0 against the engine's dump (`LSX_GC4_DUMP_LAYERS`):

| stage / layer | engine vs reference (cos) | note |
|---|---|---|
| embedding | 1.00000 | exact |
| `input_layernorm` out | 1.00000 | exact |
| GDN block out (layer 0) | 0.99597 | NVFP4 body |
| `post_self_attn_norm` + residual | 0.99668 | **0.4284 before the fix** |
| layer 0 residual | **0.99683** | **0.0757 before the fix** |

The residual-stream agreement is bounded by the NVFP4 quantization floor
(measured per-tensor `relL2 ≈ 0.094` for FP8 → NVFP4, `cos ≈ 0.9955`), not by a
semantic difference: every stage that has no quantized matmul (embedding,
norm) matches exactly, and the stages that do track it to the same floor.
`tools/gigachat4_oracle.py` runs the full-model logits comparison the same way
when a longer probe is wanted.
