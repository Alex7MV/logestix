## Publish-ready post

### Notes from an NVFP4 port of Kimi K3

We integrated Kimi K3 into our own C++/CUDA inference engine (no PyTorch at
runtime). The released checkpoint is 1.5 TB: the routed experts are already
4-bit (MXFP4, E2M1 with one E8M0 exponent per 32 columns) and everything else is
BF16. Because MXFP4 and NVFP4 share the E2M1 grid, the experts repack
**losslessly** — nibbles verbatim, each E8M0 byte duplicated into the two
16-wide halves of its 32-block, the exponent bias folded into one per-tensor
power-of-two global scale. 82 432 expert files, ~20 minutes of pure I/O, no GPU
in the expert path.

**Architecture facts that matter.** 93 layers: layer 0 is a dense SiTU MLP,
1..92 are MoE, and the attention alternates three KDA (linear) layers followed
by one gated MLA layer, with an extra MLA layer at the end (0-based
{3,7,…,91,92}). Depth is mixed by **Attention Residuals** every 12 layers: each
layer's input and MLP input are a softmax mixture over the block's residual
streams plus the running prefix, scored by an RMS-dot against a learned
per-channel vector. The MoE is a *latent* MoE: the hidden state is projected to
3584 dims, 16 of 896 experts run there, the result is RMS-normalised and
projected back, and two shared experts see the full 7168-wide hidden. The
activation is SiTU-GLU: `4·tanh(g/4)·sigmoid(g) · 25·tanh(u/25)`.

**Details that silently change the answer.** (1) The MLA layers carry
`qk_rope=64` dims but **no rotary module at all** — `mla_use_nope` means those
64 dims are plain key dims, and `k_rot` is one head broadcast across all 96.
(2) KDA's decay is channel-wise: the checkpoint stores `A_log` with 128 entries
(= `head_dim`), and the log-decay is `-5·sigmoid(exp(A_log[d])·(g_d + dt_bias))`
— the `fla` reference indexes `A_log` per head, which cannot consume a
128-entry tensor for 96 heads. (3) The router selects by `sigmoid+bias` but
weights by the *raw* sigmoid renormalised to 1. (4) `routed_expert_norm` sits
between the expert sum and the up-projection, not before the experts. (5) The
chat format is XTML, not ChatML: `<|open|>message role="user"<|sep|>body…
<|close|>message<|sep|><|end_of_msg|>`, and the model answers inside a `think`
channel, so a parity harness must cut by id or window past the reasoning.

**Measured.** SelfCheck (framed control prompt) PASS: `The answer is Paris.`
with `logits_hash=5c1e227265d6a27b`, top-1 id 1008. Tokenizer parity 23/23
against the checkpoint's own `tiktoken.model` (`tokenizer_kind 2` unchanged).
Tool codec verified byte-exactly against `encoding_k3.py`'s frames on a synthetic
tokenizer. 24.5 GB pinned + 0.43 GB KDA state + 0.11 GB latent KV at 4 k
context fits the card; the latent/shared plane streams at ~103 MiB per layer
per token and the routed experts at ~27 GB per token.

**Not measured (and why).** The source cannot execute on one 32 GB card (it
needs `fla-core` plus a multi-GPU FP8/BF16 runner), so FSI/CODE/DET goldens are
**template-derived** (`tools/kimi_k3_template_goldens.py`, the prompt set's own
Expected Template) and the three gates had not been run to completion at
publication time — at the measured ~2 s/token and 512–2048-token budgets they
are multi-hour runs. Perf rows are likewise unseeded; the decode path is
host-store bound by construction (27 GB of experts per token).
